Write a message

A note becomes an entry in four moves

  1. 01

    You talk

    Voice or text, any length, no format to follow. Send it the way you'd send it to a friend at midnight.

  2. 02

    It writes

    Transcribed, cleaned into readable prose, given a title, tags, and today's date. The original wording stays available.

  3. 03

    You edit

    Change the title, retag, move the date, reformat the text. Nothing reaches Notion until you press Save.

  4. 04

    It files

    One row in your journal database. No duplicates, no orphan pages, no scattered inbox to clean up later.

It keeps notes on you

Roast
An honest read on the entry you just sent. Reply to it and the conversation keeps going — deeper, with the rest of your diary in the room.
Profile
A private knowledge base about you, built from your own notes without you filling in a single form. It posts a short line whenever it learns something new.
Rules
Tell it to stop moralising, to answer in one line, to never mention your ex. It remembers, and the rules live on a Notion page you can edit by hand.
Storage
Entries, profile, and rules all live in your Notion. We keep nothing: no copies of your notes, no accounts, no servers of ours holding your diary.
Recaps
A daily summary lands on its own. Ask for the weekly report whenever you want the longer view.

Four things it is built for

Four reasons people keep the chat pinned.

  • Break the loop you are stuck in

    Say the story you keep telling yourself out loud. It pushes back instead of nodding along, with your earlier notes as context.

  • Think a decision through

    Talk the call out before you make it. The entry keeps what you actually weighed, so the next you can check the reasoning instead of the outcome.

  • Dump the 1 a.m. spiral

    Rant in, clean entry out. The thought stops circling once it is written down, titled, and filed somewhere you can find it.

  • Review a week without homework

    No Sunday ritual to keep up. The recap is already written from what you said during the week.

Questions people ask first

Where is my diary stored?
In your own Notion database. Running your own copy, the path is Telegram to your machine to your Notion, and nothing is kept anywhere else. The public demo bot runs on someone else's box and holds the entry only as long as it takes to write your Notion row.
Do I have to type anything?
No. Send a voice note and it is transcribed, titled, tagged, and dated for you. Typed messages work the same way if you would rather write.
What does it cost?
The code is free and open source. You pay only your own OpenAI usage and whatever the box you run it on costs.
Can I change how it talks to me?
Yes. Tell it to stop moralising or answer in one line and it keeps that as a rule. The rules live on a Notion page you can edit by hand.
Does it fit the Notion Journal template?
Yes. One entry becomes one row with title, text, tags, and date, so the journal views and filters keep working.

Run it yourself

Open source Python. Your Telegram token, your OpenAI key, your Notion database, on a box you control. Run your own copy and your notes never touch anyone else's infrastructure. Clone it, fill in .env, start the process — that's the whole install.

What you need before the first note

  • Telegram bot token from @BotFather
  • Notion integration token and a database shared with it
  • OpenAI API key
  • Python 3.11 on any always-on box — about ten minutes
